## How to use

1. **Drag and drop JPG or JPEG images** onto the box, or choose files from your device. You can add more images at any time — new images are added to the end of the list.
2. **Check the order.** The images become PDF pages in the order shown, top to bottom. Use the up and down arrows next to an image to move it, or the × button to remove it. **Clear all** empties the list and starts over.
3. **Set an output name**, or leave it blank for the default, `images.pdf`. An unsafe or empty name is cleaned up automatically rather than rejected.
4. Select **Download PDF**. The images are converted in your browser and the result downloads immediately — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

**What JPG to PDF does:** it converts the JPG or JPEG images you select into a single PDF, one image per page, in the order you choose. It does not edit, crop, rotate, or compress the images — each page is simply sized to match its own image.

## Method

**Images are processed in your browser where supported.** No file upload is required for this tool — every image you add is read and converted on your own device, and the PDF is generated and downloaded without ever leaving it.

**Image order and page behavior.** Each image becomes its own page, sized to that image's own dimensions — there is no cropping, no scaling to a fixed paper size, and no image editing. This keeps every page an exact, predictable match for its source image. One known limitation: if a photo was taken in portrait mode with orientation information written by the camera rather than the pixels themselves rotated, that image may appear in its original, unrotated orientation on the page. Verify the generated PDF before relying on it.

**File size and browser limits.** Each image can be up to 15 MB, the combined total up to 75 MB, and up to 50 images can be converted at once. Very large images, too many images, or a corrupted image file may fail in the browser — these are honest, stated limits for a tool that runs entirely on your device, not a server.

**Common errors.** A specific, plain-English message explains exactly what went wrong: no images selected, a file that isn't a JPG or JPEG image, an image or combined total over the size limit, more than 50 images, or a corrupted image that can't be read. Do not use this tool for images you are not allowed to process.

## FAQ

### Is JPG to PDF free?

Yes. It's free and unlimited, with no sign-up and no ads.

### Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Every image you add is read and converted in your own browser. No file upload is required for this tool.

### What order do the images convert in?

The order shown in the list, top to bottom. Use the up and down arrows to reorder an image before converting.

### Does JPG to PDF support PNG or other image formats?

Not in this version. Only JPG and JPEG images are supported; other formats return a clear error rather than a silently wrong result.

### Will the PDF page match my photo's orientation?

Usually, but not always. Each page is sized to the image's own pixel dimensions. A photo whose orientation comes only from camera metadata, rather than from the pixels themselves, may appear unrotated on the page. Verify the generated PDF before sending or publishing it.

### Why did my image fail to convert?

The most common reasons are a corrupted or truncated image file, a file that isn't actually a JPG or JPEG, or an image or combined total over the stated size limit. The error message names the specific problem and, where relevant, the file.

### Is there a file size limit?

Yes: 15 MB per image, 75 MB combined, and up to 50 images. These limits are stated honestly because everything runs in your browser, not on a server.
